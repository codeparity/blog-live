Key findings from normalized analysis

🪂 Finding 1 — Amar wins on personal carbon at every single phase
Across all four normalized life phases, Amar records the lowest carbon in every row.
The gap is widest in the career and family phases where Anthony's Gulf lifestyle and beef-heavy Kerala diet push his carbon to 62 and 78 tCO₂ respectively — nearly double Amar's 34 and 52. Amar's Sannyasa stage in later life is a structurally unique carbon drawdown that neither Christianity nor Islam prescribes, taking his elder phase carbon to the lowest of the three.

✝ Finding 2 — Anthony wins on lineage carbon despite highest personal carbon
The paradox: Anthony has the worst personal carbon (176 tCO₂) yet his lineage produces the least cumulative carbon at 80 years (2,831 tCO₂).
Sub-replacement fertility (TFR 1.8) means his carbon doesn't multiply — it effectively caps.
The Kerala model concentrates wealth and carbon in fewer, wealthier individuals rather than diluting it across a large tree.

☀ Finding 3 — Akbar's TFR creates a compounding divergence that overwhelms all other variables

By generation 2 (~40 years), Akbar's lineage has already overtaken Amar's in both carbon and liability, despite Akbar individually being more frugal. By generation 4, the gap is 31,000 vs 2,831 tCO₂ vs Anthony — an 11× difference driven almost entirely by TFR 3.6 compounding to 168 people.
No individual lifestyle intervention can bridge this gap.
The qurbani goat (🦘 ₹15K/yr mandatory) and Hajj (₹4.5L obligatory) both scale linearly with population, amplifying the effect.

📍 Finding 4 — Pilgrimage carbon is not proportional to religious obligation
Hajj is religiously obligatory (fard) — Char Dham and Jerusalem pilgrimages are voluntary acts of devotion. Yet the carbon cost of Hajj (~8 tCO₂) is 4× Jerusalem and 40× Char Dham.
The mandatory nature of Hajj means this carbon cost is structurally embedded in Islamic practice in a way that no equivalent obligation exists in Hinduism or Christianity.

🎊 Finding 5 — Festival costs normalize differently: Anthony pays the most annually, Akbar pays the most per ritual event

On a normalized per-year basis, Anthony spends ₹62K/yr on festivals — driven by the church tithe (10% of income) more than any single celebration. Akbar's ₹52K/yr is dominated by two Eid celebrations plus the annual qurbani goat. Amar's ₹38K/yr is spread across a larger number of smaller festivals.

However, Akbar's single highest one-time ritual cost (Hajj ₹4.5L) exceeds any single Hindu or Christian obligation of equivalent religious necessity.
