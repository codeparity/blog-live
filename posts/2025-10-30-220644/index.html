"Red Father" was a little-known, rules-based program at Bell Labs in the mid-1970s, notable for its mystique and obscurity compared to more famous early chatbots.

The story of "Red Father"

Location and era: The chatbot existed in the mid-1970s at the Bell Labs research facility in Murray Hill, New Jersey.

Origin: The chatbot was likely a passion project created by a Bell Labs researcher. The meaning of its name is unknown, though some have speculated it was a Cold War reference.

Interaction: Users communicated with "Red Father" through a keyboard and received responses via text on a teletype terminal connected to a mainframe computer. Amy Feldman, who used the bot as a child, recalled that its conversational objective seemed to be a game of keeping it engaged as long as possible before it would retort, "Go talk to your mother".

Obscurity: Feldman later tried to find information about the chatbot but was unsuccessful despite contacting AT&T's corporate historian and retired employees. This suggests the project was never intended for a wider audience or commercial use.

Comparison with other 1970s chatbots
While "Red Father" faded into obscurity, it was part of a larger, more documented era of AI chatbot development in the 1970s.

ELIZA (1966): Created by Joseph Weizenbaum at MIT, ELIZA was the first chatbot. It used a "pattern-matching and substitution" technique and famously mimicked a Rogerian psychotherapist in its "DOCTOR" script.

PARRY (1972): Developed by psychiatrist Kenneth Colby at Stanford, PARRY was a more advanced chatbot that simulated a person with paranoid schizophrenia. In a 1972 test, psychiatrists could distinguish PARRY from human patients only about 48% of the time, highlighting its more sophisticated, rules-based conversational engine.

SHRDLU (1970): Terry Winograd's natural language program was able to interact with users in plain English to manipulate objects in a virtual "block world." It was an early achievement in natural language processing but was limited to a specific, structured environment.

CYRUS (1978-1983): The CYRUS project created a chatbot that simulated U.S. Secretary of State Cyrus Vance. It used case-based reasoning and updated its database by parsing news wire services.
